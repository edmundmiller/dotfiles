# Source notes

Richard W. Hamming, _The Art of Doing Science and Engineering: Learning to Learn_,
Stripe Press, 2020. References use EPUB chapter numbers and passage anchors,
not invented page numbers. Primary text was read in `OEBPS/Text/Chapter25.xhtml`
and `OEBPS/Text/Chapter26.xhtml`. The private book is not bundled or required at
runtime.

## Chapter 26: Experts

- **Passage following the water-lifting example:** Hamming argues that
  impossibility claims depend on assumptions which experts may neglect to
  reexamine. We adapt this into checking a claim's scope and load-bearing
  assumptions. The historical anecdote itself has not been independently checked.
- **Discussion of his missed FFT opportunity:** he identifies technological
  change as a reason formerly sensible reasoning may cease to apply. We adapt
  this into checking what changed rather than presuming old advice is obsolete.
- **Passage on expertise and certainty:** “What would you accept as evidence
  you are wrong?” We apply this symmetrically to the established claim and the
  proposed alternative, including the agent's own preferred explanation.
- **Discussion of outsiders with unconventional ideas:** he warns against both
  dismissing all such ideas and spending a career chasing them. The skill's
  bounded investigation is an adaptation of that tension.
- **Closing discussion of his stories:** he explicitly qualifies his theories
  as nonabsolute observations. His broader statements about experts are not
  grounds for dismissing expertise or treating dissent as proof.

## Chapter 25: Creativity

- **Passage beginning “When stuck”:** he suggests considering the shape of a
  solution, conservation laws, symmetry, and the necessity of assumptions.
- **Analogy discussion after the account of insight:** he describes analogies
  as suggestive and partial, and warns against pressing them too far. We use
  analogies to propose checks, never to establish that an alternative works.

## What we added

The numbered workflow, evidence-status distinctions, output format, authority
boundaries, symmetric tests, and examples are our agent-specific design choices.
Hamming does not provide this exact checklist. This skill does not claim agents
have human subconscious incubation, nor that a prompt guarantees creativity.
