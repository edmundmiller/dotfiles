# Source notes

Richard W. Hamming, _The Art of Doing Science and Engineering: Learning to Learn_,
Stripe Press, 2020. Chapter 27, “Unreliable Data,” EPUB
`OEBPS/Text/Chapter27.xhtml`. Passage anchors replace edition-dependent page numbers.

- **Opening life-testing discussion and the telephone-call recording anecdote:**
  Hamming questions the measurement apparatus, including a machine's records
  about itself. We adapt this into tracing collection rather than treating an
  aggregate or instrument label as sufficient evidence.
- **Western Electric inventory study followed by the personnel-study pilot:**
  he describes residual inconsistent records and collection changing between
  pilot and production. These motivate consistency and collection-path checks.
- **Computer-company pricing decision, after the discussion of model error:**
  he warns that uncertain sales guesses inherited the apparent reliability of
  careful engineering estimates. We adapt this into tracking uncertainty through
  the decision, not merely through the best-measured input.
- **Inventory tax-reporting change and subsequent time-series discussion:**
  he points to changing definitions as a source of apparent trends. This motivates
  checking comparability, not assuming every trend is an artifact.
- **Closing advice on collection, small carefully selected samples, questionnaires,
  and heterogeneous respondents:** he emphasizes collection quality and asks how
  conclusions could be justified. We adapt this into decision-relevant sampling,
  missingness, and population checks, not a rule that smaller samples are better.

## Adaptation and limits

The decision threshold, denominator accounting, sampling correction example,
sensitivity check, stopping rule, and authority boundaries are our additions.
The 4%/3% example is synthetic, not from Hamming. Chapter 29 also discusses how
instruments select what is seen; score incentives are handled by the separate
metrics pilot rather than repeated here.

Hamming opens by acknowledging examples and generalizations rather than a
coherent theory. His numerical rule about published accuracies is explicitly
qualified as an exaggeration, not a calibrated error multiplier. We do not adopt
it, his broad claims about disciplines or poverty, or his historical polling
accounts as established facts. Nor do we endorse deception to obtain remeasurement
in his anecdote. The private book is not bundled or required at runtime.
